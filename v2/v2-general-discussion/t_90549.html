PvP: Horde Commander Mode?

1 Posts • 156 Views
E
Ensrick • 2024-01-07 18:28:19

In a Horde Commander vs. Invader PvP mode, players would engage in dynamic and shifting missions. One team takes on the role of our usual heroes as invaders, while the other team assumes the role of horde commanders defending and spawning waves of enemies. They can summon bosses to hinder the attackers’ progress and even directly engage the players by spawning in and playing as unique Skaven or Chaos champions with their unique abilities. This concept could be applied to routine linear-style missions or possibly missions with checkpoint objectives—there are many possibilities.

I recommend integrating the difficulty options from the original campaign mode, along with the weapons, items, and talents used in normal campaign missions, into such a game mode. The issue with Winds of Magic’s weaves lies in the awards and gains not translating well to the normal campaign mode. Additionally, cosmetic rewards from PvP and the campaign mode should be interchangeable. Without this interchangeability, there will be almost no incentive for players like me who primarily play the campaign mode and prefer co-op.

Here’s a simplified example of how this mode might work:

1. The Heroes/Invaders:

  • A 4-player co-op team.
  • As usual, the primary goal is to survive and complete a mission while battling waves of enemies and bosses from the defending team, all while achieving the mission’s objectives.
  • Defeating an enemy commander and completing mission objectives/checkpoints might offer unique buffs and supplies (potions, ammo, bombs).

2. The Enemies / Defenders:

  • A 4-player team, with two Chaos and Skaven commanders each.
  • The commanders’ goal is not only to defeat the heroes but to hinder the heroes’ progress or prevent them from completing their objectives.
  • Each enemy commander has a limited supply that replenishes over time, which can be spent on various actions. These actions have cooldowns and limitations on simultaneous effects/hordes.
  • The key to victory for enemy commanders is effective resource management and synergization.

3. Commanding Enemy Hordes:

  • The enemy commanders each have a limited supply number that replenishes over time, which can be spent on various actions.
  • Actions could be divided among commanders, with each responsible for different buffs, debuffs, mission modifiers, and spawning different types of enemies. For instance, one Skaven commander might spawn assassins and gas rats, while the other can spawn warpfire, gunners, or hooks.
  • At certain checkpoints or arenas in the mission, players have the opportunity to spawn as their chosen champion. There might even be an option for one of the two chaos champions to be a Beastman. Multiple champions can spawn simultaneously for final objectives.

4. Gameplay:

  • As the mission progresses, the difficulty increases with more challenging waves of enemies and faster supply replenishment for the horde commanders.
  • To avoid having the game end too quickly, defeating the invading heroes outright won’t be so easy, it will require careful timing, synergizing, and resource management. I recommend giving the heroes a certain number of “lives” to start with that can be used to respawn back in action and the possibility of gaining them after beating enemy champions or completing objectives.
  • Besides the main objective of winning or losing, I recommend having several minor objectives. These objectives, which the heroes can either succeed or fail at, contribute to awards for both teams. Achievements could include completing tasks within a certain time, carrying specific items in inventory slots throughout the mission, killing particular enemy commanders, or completing unique challenges for Okri’s book.

5. Rewards:

  • Obviously, there’s a winner and a loser, but it’s also good to weigh progress and have minor objectives count towards rewards for both sides. This ensures that even the loser earns rewards for their progress.
  • Rewards might include the usual silver coins for the shop, hero experience, and vaults. However, there are other potential awards to incentivize players further:
    • Unlocking unique enemy champions for PvP.
    • Attaining new special cosmetic weapon skins, outfits, and frames by playing this game mode.
    • Earning points or reward currency that can be saved up or stored to spend on cosmetics, similar to shillings.

This type of game mode adds an extra layer of strategy and intensity to the typical horde gameplay, as players must contend not only with AI-controlled enemies but also with the unpredictable actions of the opposing team controlling formidable foes and champions. It encourages teamwork, coordination, and adaptability as players switch between the roles of hero vs enemy commanders throughout the match.

My Thoughts on Why Other Possible PvP Modes Would Likely Fail:
I believe the PvP concept has likely been set in stone since its announcement. Despite this, I’ve put forward my suggestion, and it is probably the only way I would find PvP appealing. Competitive modes like 4v4 teams, capture the flag, arena combat, horde survival, resource race, etc., would only interest me to the extent I could obtain a unique weapon skin since I’m a pay piggie and like shiny new cosmetics.

PvP games always face enormous challenges in careful balancing due to complex factors that can’t easily be accounted for. Fatshark might prove me wrong, but they have demonstrated they don’t understand their game well from a player’s perspective.

They featured players from Twitch and YouTube like Wet Magic, who often misrepresent basics in tutorials and are way below what you could expect from many of the better players on YouTube and Twitch like RoyaleWithCheese, Core, ChocoB, GuzThe, Velsix, etc.

The recent balance pass introduced significant issues, resulting in a substantial buff to the Engineer, making him the game’s top career. It seems like they can’t invest the resources necessary to prevent reintroducing bugs and comprehensively cover community feedback. Even when they do review feedback, they don’t seem to have a lot of people there who understand their own game well enough from a player’s perspective to put community feedback in perspective.

It’s just hard for me to imagine they could pull off a decent PvP mode, assuming it’s akin to your team’s PvP straight-up combat, but we shall see.
