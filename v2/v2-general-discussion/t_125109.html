I think people are using “bad” differently here. I used 1h Axe extensively before the patch, and I still use Greathammer quite often. I would describe both as specialized and sometimes comparatively inefficient, but not bad overall.

Greathammer’s heavies trade kill speed for exceptional crowd control. They stagger extremely well and keep moving through dense, armor-mixed groups, while its lights provide strong single-target and armored damage. Its real problem is comparative efficiency: with enough power and stagger investment, other weapons can reach many of the same useful stagger breakpoints while also killing more quickly. That can make Greathammer suboptimal beside certain Kruber and Bardin options, but suboptimal is not the same as nonfunctional. I would not confuse Crowd Control with Crowd Clearing. They’re 2 different things. Again, the problem is that builds can be designed easily so that Crowd Clearing weapons do enough stagger that main-purpose crowd control weapons are often just crutches to help people survive better, and Greathammer is requires careful timing so you don’t get poked between heavy attacks, so it’s not even that great at that, and you sacrifice the dodge count/distance of other 1h weapons that can stagger like Mace/Hammer. I guess if one were to buff it, just give all it’s attacks a 20% damage boost or something.

Likewise, the 1h Axe had a legitimate use. Its principal weakness was cleave, but it already had strong anti-armor damage, good defensive statistics and +10% critical chance on its lights. On Slayer it gets around 35% crit chance on lights; even before the buff, it was devastating. Ironbreaker and Ranger Veteran could pair it with Drakefire weapons or the Grudge-Raker to cover its poor crowd clear. You mentioned using melee careers to pick a weapon to offset their weaknesses, but the point is, there’s nothing stopping you from doing that with ranged weapons too.

I agree that Axe having low cleave in a horde game made it a lot less useful, but I think the total package in the recent patch went too far. So far, it’s received additional cleave, better movement while attacking, greater dodge range and stronger armored and super-armored heavies. That substantially reduced its defining weakness while leaving its crit, mobility and single-target strengths intact. I would not say it categorically outclasses Dual Axes or Axe & Falchion, but I think 1H axe generally is now just better than Falchion, and I daresay it’s close to making Greataxe seem pretty obsolete. 1h Axe is now OP for sure, it just needed a small cleave boost for handling trash, even then, it was one of my most used weapons on Bounty Hunter, RV, and Ironbreaker for the way it pairs nicely with CC ranged.

The same design logic applies to Hammer & Tome. I agree that its ordinary lights have essentially no cleave. That is intentional, and it is not the entirety of the weapon’s crowd-control kit. The push attack creates space, while charged H2 provides the large-area stagger and density payoff. Those tools cost stamina, positioning and timing, as they should. Warrior Priest also has a second melee slot, so Hammer & Tome does not need clear through crows with light attack spam.

With pre-charging and careful positioning, Heavy 2 is not merely a rare highlight involving fifty conveniently stacked enemies. It can be used repeatedly throughout ordinary dense engagements. Light attack 1 into Heavy 2 is the weapon’s intended routing to get that juicy powerful explosion attack. Evaluating it primarily through light spam or uncharged-heavy spam ignores the part of the moveset carrying its specialization.

So I do not think resembling the old 1h Axe automatically means Hammer & Tome deserves the same changes. Tome already possesses the multi-target attacks, which 1h Axe lacked.

The Flail and Shield I’d argue is OP, it’s pretty blatantly abusable. I think making it’s light attacks have a little more cleave, and then making the heavy windup slower would be a good way to balance both Flail and Shield, and the regular Flail. Also, might consider tweaking the dodge distance/count of each.

On WP, the only weapon I tend to get similar damage output compared to Skull-Splitter and Tome is the Reckoner, but not quite. The Skull-Splitter and Tome tends to be an easy (albeit a bit boring) way to get max damage. With timing, positioning, and practice, that thing clears out hordes. You have 1k hours, I have 4k hours and I still learn new things. I’ve confidently blundered into these forums about buffs/nerfs only to completely change my mind after investing more time. It’s true that with more time investment, you can learn to make anything work, but I can confidently say that the Skull-Splitter and Tome is the #1 weapon on WP for racking up damage numbers, and the only reason I wouldn’t say nerf it, is because it’s not a capable weapon on it’s own. I don’t have the spreadsheet, but I used the source code for some calculations. On the surface, the numbers are good, but that’s not the full story.

Falchion H1-L2-L3 does produce approximately 29.8 armour DPS. But Axe L1+QQ produces approximately 28. The Axe’s ordinary L1-L2-L3 chain is approximately 25, so it appears you may be comparing the Axe’s basic light chain against the Falchion’s optimized single-target combination.

That is already a much smaller difference: roughly 28.1 versus 29.8 before accounting for Swift Slaying or the usual attack-speed property. It certainly does not demonstrate that Axe’s single-target performance was merely average, much less that the complete weapon was “awful.”

All three old Axe lights had an attack-specific +10% critical chance. With the conventional setup, that means:

5% base + 5% weapon + 5% trinket + 10% Axe light = 25% crit.

Slayer’s crit talent raises that to 35%. Swift Slaying gives 20% attack speed for five seconds and refreshes on every critical hit. Using the actual pre-patch attack timings, 25% crit gives roughly 94–96% sustained Swift Slaying uptime while spamming Axe lights. At 35%, it is approximately 99%. That effectively gives you about 30 DPS with axe.

Falchion also benefits from Swift Slaying, but it begins at 15% rather than 25% under the same property setup and therefore has appreciably lower uptime. A comparison that uses neither the common +5% attack-speed property nor Swift Slaying disproportionately undervalues the Axe’s hidden crit bonus.

Those values are in the old 1H Axe source and Swift Slaying definition.

The spreadsheet also admittedly omits stagger and practical attack uptime. Those omissions are especially important here. Axe lights had substantial first-target impact, and against armour a critical light changed the impact modifier from 0.5 to 1.0. With Enhanced Power, which I frequently used, the additional 7.5% power increased both damage and impact, though it arguably benefits more from Smiter or Assassin.

In practice, my Axe lights could repeatedly stagger Stormvermin and Maulers. That allowed me to continue attacking, produce more critical hits and keep Swift Slaying running. I could and did fight entire Stormvermin patrols in melee with the pre-patch Axe. Its heavies also gave it a decent answer to super armour and Chaos Warriors.
