Silly Fighting Things

I’d only swat at a spear like that if I was the aggressor; if I’m flat footed or falling back, I’d always take a centered guard for defense. If your blade is to your right and the spear strikes at your right rib-cage, the only way you can push the spear with your weapon is… left. That’ll take it across all of your vitals and, if it’s moving with any form of momentum, it’ll still find its mark. Most fighters I’ve fought with and watched only perform a parry like that when it’s involving a similarly weighted slashing weapon (sword-sword or foil-foil) or when a sword is running down a spear. Again, though, that’s on offense when the sword is charging and the spear needs to be out of the way.

Let me just reiterate; a good spearman can hold multiple swordsmen at bay because it’s a longer weapon with next to zero wind-up. All you need is a push and a pull motion, and by striking with it (swing motion) it breaks a passive guard, but is defeated by an active guard.

A spearman that gets surrounded by 2 swordsmen is a bad spearman, because the way to win is to just keep stabbing, keep them busy and they won’t have time to attack because they simply cannot reach you, while you can just stab away.
There are very good reasons why the spear was the most used weapon with the highest kill-ratio in historical times.
