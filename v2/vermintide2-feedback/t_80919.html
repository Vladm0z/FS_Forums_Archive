Feedback & Suggestions for Improving Experience Under Poor Connectivity Conditions

Having spent a significant amount of time playing Vermintide 2 under various network conditions, I’ve seen firsthand how even minor increases in latency can have a dramatic effect on gameplay. It’s an issue that unfairly penalizes non-hosting players, leading to a range of problems that can detract from the enjoyment of the game. I’ve played on Fiber Internet with gigabit speeds and extremely low latency, as well as USB Tethering from my phone’s hotspot with speeds averaging 20-25 megabits. I have both hosted and joined other players for countless hours under both of these scenarios.

This is far from an isolated issue. Many players experience these difficulties, which risk discouraging engagement and can, over time, drive players away from the game. Considering this is a long-time issue that continues, it is evident that solutions to these issues seem to be lacking.

Feedback on Latency
The main issue caused by high latency is the desynchronization or skipping of enemy attack animations. This can severely disrupt the player’s ability to respond to threats and can also impede the development of muscle memory and adaptive gameplay strategies.

Suggestion for Latency Issues
If dedicated servers, netcode optimization, and interpolation are not feasible or have proven ineffective in improving latency-related issues, an alternative approach could involve the game itself dynamically adjusting for latency. While not an ideal solution, this adaptive approach could serve as a valuable measure to mitigate the impact of latency and enhance the overall player experience by providing more responsive gameplay despite suboptimal network conditions.
I suggest a damage delay system that takes into account a player’s current ping. This system would provide additional reaction time for players experiencing high latency, reducing their inherent disadvantage and ensuring a more engaging and equitable gaming experience.
As latency increases, the onset of taking a hit could be appropriately delayed, allowing players more time to react. Conversely, the onset of delivering a hit could be slightly accelerated, ensuring smoother interactions between players, especially in situations with higher latency. The same could apply to other actions that take time where small increments matter.

Feedback on Disconnect or Unstable Connection
Players currently experience situations where they take damage while disconnecting and reconnecting, and interactions with objects often lead to unintended glitches and inconsistencies in the game. This seems to happen during packet loss, or object interaction in-game such as picking up items/ammo or interacting with objectives (whether done by host or player).

Suggestion for Disconnect or Unstable Connection
The game client’s existing ability to detect impending disconnects could also be leveraged to temporarily suspend that player’s game world interactions. This could prevent them from taking damage or causing game-breaking interactions during the period of network instability. I don’t see why players who seem to be disconnecting/reconnecting have to endure taking hits or potentially glitching objectives. There has to be a way to suspend their interactions with the game until the connection restabilizes. They shouldn’t be running around taking damage while unable to do anything.

If we don’t address these issues, they will continue to tarnish the Vermintide 2 experience. They inhibit skill development, undermine strategies, and most importantly, sap the fun out of the game. Furthermore, these problems risk creating a steep entry barrier for newcomers who are met with challenges tied to network constraints, not just gameplay difficulty. I’ve seen a number of new players who have to deal with this issue, and quickly realize the peer-to-peer hosting latency issues are game-breaking if you want to experience higher difficulties and don’t have a stable connection.

Addressing these issues isn’t just a pleasantry or quality of life improvement- it’s vital for the health of the game and its community. Improving Vermintide 2’s handling of network instability and latency will make the game more accessible and enjoyable for everyone, regardless of their network conditions.

I believe it’s long overdue that we draw more attention to these issues because it’s easy to ignore and assume that latency just is latency, but I think this may be the single most significant issue facing the game, and that there must be ways to improve it. Anyone got any other ideas?
