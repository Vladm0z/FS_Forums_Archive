The games may appear mostly the same at a glance but they are quite different.

  1. If you like higher movespeed, there are builds&careers that move faster, at times quite a lot so, than many others but yes, its more limited by default. Our characters be conserving stamina.

  2. Dodge-mobility is highly based on the weapon in question, and to some extent, on the career using it. The Elf, especially the Handmaiden career, typically has more dodges&better dodge distance at the expense of stagger, cleave and durability.

The opposite end of this spectrum is Kruber, typically slow, heavy and good at wide strong swings.

  1. No audio ques from behind is…kind of a bug, i can only suggest fighting with your back to a wall or some other such object when possible. Positioning is important.

  2. Healing is basically a skill issue most of the time, a large part of the game for melee is both the skill to avoid damage and the ability to generate “white health” or “temporary health” by doing something determined by your careers lvl 5 row. Like killing or staggering enemies among others. A proficient fighter basically generates more of this health than the enemies can deal to him under ordinary circumstances.

  3. Getting downed means you enter a “bleeding out” state where your THP decays and you die if you take a second hit that would down you. The way to cure this is either healing yourself with a medkit/healing pot or healing someone else with a medkit and then go generate enough THP to offset the decay.

It’s weird to me to say dodging is underpowered in VT2. Unlike DT it doesn’t pause stamina regen which means you can combine dodging and pushing WAY more.

Also unlike DT stam regen property on trinket BOTH increases stam regen speed AND reduces delay before regen starts. BCR also feels more powerful so it’s quite easy to make stamina management orders of magnitude more forgiving than DT.

Sprinting and sliding have no place in this game, they would make it way too trivial to avoid enemies altogether when you want to, and you can already kite very effectively on decent mobility weapons with just a little terrain use. That’s part of the skill curve. It’s fun.

THP in this game is also insanely strong on any frontliner class which makes enemy damage a lot more forgiving than you’re making out. If you’re struggling with burst damage barkskin exists and is quite popular to defend against it.

This really all reads as someone who has made no effort to understand the mechanics of the game complaining it’s not identical to DT. There’s a lot of crossover but it’s not entirely the same skill set or approach to encounters. Both have their merits, VT2 feels a bit more methodical and calculated which is its own fun. Both games give you quite a lot of tools to approach encounters, you’ll get a feel for it with time.
