Agayek • 2026-07-22 15:41:30
Ethics:

So my question is, why do FS devs refuse to implement community made fixes to the games technical and usability related issues? Is it arrogance? Ignorance? Or simply spite?

Most likely, any community changes not being added to the base game is a mix of a) the devs disagreeing on its value for whatever reason, b) the community actually being wrong, c) the devs not having the time or cycles to spare to integrate, test, refine, and iterate community fixes, and d) the existence of mods that contain the solution meaning players can just install the mod and the devs don’t feel like they have to worry about it any further.

Fatshark’s dev cycle has always been very slow, for better or worse, and it’s not entirely unreasonable for them to be reluctant to set aside whatever plans they’ve laid for that time and to instead analyze, adapt, and integrate community ideas and changes into the game.

It would certainly be very nice if they did; Lord knows I’ve put enough effort into futzing with the game that I would appreciate some of it being realized. But there’s also very reasonable reasons why it can take a long time for such things to work their way through the CICD pipeline.

Metaphage:

It’s a feature that would need maintenance still. Consider that at some point in Vermintide 2’s lifetime FS has decided to ditch maintaining the PC UI in favour of a more console-friendly look, because they couldn’t warrant upkeeping two looks, for two different platforms.

That’s not to say it would apply in the same way, but either way, it would be a lot of extra localisation, and while it is value added to players - mind you a minority, too - it’s also a cost to upkeep.

I’m in the boat that gives the community of dedicated people agency and community-driven resources over how much they need to know about certain skills or effects or interactions, especially since that would indirectly and figuratively make the devs spend less time on upkeeping it and more time on actually developing the game, and people an opportunity to mentor other people (or themselves). The devs weren’t interested in enclosing too much information to their players in the first place, if some of it wasn’t there from the get-go.
