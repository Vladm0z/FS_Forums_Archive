Winning two games raises you by two ranks, while losing five lowers you by one rank, leaving you just one step away from a second drop. So if you win twice out of every seven games, that results in a systematic increase in rank.

The odds of success in a single match are poor precisely because, in the current system, rank growth stops for a player who loses three times for every victory. If rank dropped after every single defeat, the chances of winning any given match would already rise to 50–50. And if, for example, the system were reversed—three wins required for a rank increase, but only one loss for a rank drop—then the chance of success in a single match would rise to 75%.
