I did a quick little analysis I wanted to share, in light of some of the balance discussions of late, with respect to the relative strength of the Ogryn and Arbitrator relative to vet in particular.

Here’s a quick analysis that tabulates how many talent points it takes cumulatively to reach each key element via the least cost path with consideration of longest paths as well

image802×222 6.89 KB

Basically when looking at the least cost path between the major elements (blitz, aura, ability, and keystone), there are a few things that stand out.

The Arbiter has great flexible with it costing minimally 12 points to hit any combination of blitz, aura, and ability. Granted ,the other classes can technically hit it in less (11 for Vet, Psyker, and Ogryn) but that’s only if you go straight down the quickest path. Moving back and forth can incur anywhere from +1 to +4 point cost. In short, Arbiter pays the same regardless of how much back and forth they go, so there is no opportunity to cost at all for switching sides of the tree.

Overall, Arbiter and Ogryn can both get to a keystone in as little as 15 or 14 points respectively. Vet and Zealot take 17 at the minimum, Vet sometimes 18 depending on what keystone they want. Psyker is in the middle at 16 for the shortest path, 17 depending on what keystone is selected, and potentially up to 20 when moving across the tree.

The issue with Vet, and to a lesser extent the Zealot, is that moving back and forth in the tree at the higher levels quickly incurs additional point costs. In the case of vet, this point cost coupled with the high cost of getting to certain keystones, plus how many sub-nodes the keystones have, makes it a challenge to take vet keystones without limiting other elements of the build.

Vet, and to a similar degree zealot, need to have some centralizing nodes higher in the tree that reduce the cost of moving back and forth between sides of the tree. That will help immensly with build variety and options.

look at the trees. the modern trees of ogryn and arbites have a more grid-like structure with many interconnects that enable players to move between the paths, thus creating more build variety. that’s something fatshark has visibly improved. compare them to the old trees of vet and zealot, where you don’t have as many choices after choosing a path (anyway without wasting points), and there are more “mandatory” vs “not great” abilities, severely restricting what a player can take.
