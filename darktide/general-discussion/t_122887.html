  • Whoever had the most damage in high-pressure moments
  • Whoever avoided most damage and incapacitations (net, pounce, downed) over the full duration of a mission
  • Whoever avoided the most damage and incapacitations in high-pressure moments

Hi, if you’re actually interested in stats and deep analysis of what I’ve been creating through an AI side project of scoreboard tracking. I’ll message you and share you the link and allow you access with a few others. I’m still BUILDING this model however I can share some things that the AI sees as “value”

The biggest one is the overall “OFFENSE SCORE” bracket which includes:

Melee/ranged elite kills

Overall lesser kills

Boss damage

Weak spot/crit

Damage/overkill (overkill is bad in most cases)

specialist kills

Disabler kills

What my AI model has found (from other users in this community that I’ve talked to in direct messages) and taking scoreboards from havoc 40 players and normal players who run difficulties like damnstion or heresy… is that regardless of skill level, the math equation remains the same.

if one player on the team had a 200 offense score, it means he’s essentially performing the duty of two players. The code deduced that in a perfect world of 4 equally skillled players the offense score metric would be 99-101 variance. However, because this isn’t reliable and the quality of your teammates is up to RNG gods, the variation data is way more lenient at around 80-120 variation. What this means is in any game you play if you are around 80-120 you’re doing your “share” or you’re playing at an average level

If you’re under 80, you’re getting carried. This is where a player needs to re-evaluate his build, or look at his stats and gameplay footage to understand why he’s behind. When someone has a 200+ (I myself have been 300+ offense scores at time) it doesn’t mean “this player is a god!” It’s more of a diagnosis, and means you have players who are not participating at the level required for this difficulty thus the strongest player is left to absorb the remaining pressure.

Lowest damage taken doesn’t always mean what it means either. If you’re the lowest damage take but the lowest offense score it just simply means you barely participated and forced the team to absorb the pressure you should have been absorbing.

offense score in its totality is less of a measure of skill, and a measure of responsibility and expectation, not how good you are, but are you doing the tasks assigned to you through the class you play efficiently?

The AI model also deduced that team score could mean a lot, or be completely meaningless since it gets inflated by “pickup stats” like diamantine and the scrolls or grim/scripts and other junk so it filters out most of it and it actually uses its own metric system focusing on stats that matter to get you from point A to point B.

Defense score is where the model had trouble still, it keeps thinking low damage taken is absolutely amazing, i keep showing it scores where low damage taken can sometimes just be bad participation by the offense category.

Hope this helps.

Edit: I also wanted to explain the idea of clutching and how the scoreboard is viewed through an AI. The AI cannot see that “John smith was the last remaining member and ran to the next pick up point barely survive 10 crushers and managed to pick up two people saving the run” while that’s impressive, the AI determines your skill based on math and way less probability. So in that example, if John Smith managed to clutch the round, but the scoreboard showed he had the lowest offense score performance, then it could also be determined that the cause of the wipe to begin with is due to either his lack of involvement with damage, killing, or impacting the pacing/momentum of the game. The opposite can also be true, a high offense scoring player with the highest damage dealt may mean they are pushing the game forward, which means they have high tempo, which my AI values highly because high tempo = director gets less turns to commit to different pressure categories against the player so long as players are in coherency

#1 rule is stay alive. You cannot help while down or dead. Best ways to stay alive are 1) Position well, 2) Dodge and 3) Kill the things before they kill you. Too much alive for too long will eventually overwhelm your position and ability to dodge.

Builds should account for killing: Horde, Specials, Shooters, Armor, Bosses. Good team comps have at least one build that is really good at each + whatever modifiers are in play.

Out of the presented options, I lean staying alive during high pressure, with the caveat that it should not be at the expense of your team. It indicates good awareness and consistency that can easily apply to less intense moments.
