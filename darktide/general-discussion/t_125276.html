So, a group of unpleasant individuals has appeared on the forum, I won’t point fingers, who mock players for their desire to play meta and see players with meta builds in their team. This is presented as something shameful and unworthy. But I will explain where this desire comes from.

Last fall, at rank 21, I suffered about 30 defeats in a row, playing as a Zealot with FotF, at a time when Supermen were playing with grey weapons, green curios, and empty talent trees. And my teammates were not Supermen, so defeat after defeat followed, throwing me back to rank 20, which was unpleasant due to guaranteed visual modifiers. I managed to break out of this losing streak only by using the Chorus of Spiritual Fortitude.

This summer, playing more or less meta (I don’t know where to find a notarized meta) I reached Assignment Rank 33, but that was the end of it. I got stuck; I found that out of eight games I only successfully complete one. If I am the lobby creator, I very carefully select teammates; I don’t scrutinize builds under a microscope, but I try to assemble a team according to the principle: two damage dealers of different classes (or at least with different ability and weapon sets), two tanks of different classes (or at least with different ability and weapon sets), one of which is my Zealot with Chorus (I don’t even know if it’s a tank, but it seems closer to a tank than a damage dealer) and, more importantly, I carefully look at teammates’ Assignment Rank (using mods). And even so, I suffered three defeats in a row at rank 33, three defeats in a row at rank 32, and three defeats in a row at rank 31. In my last ranked game with randoms, I barely successfully completed a rank 31 mission, clutching at the final event with a Zealot with Chorus and Relic Blade (not the best combination for clutching). That was possibly the peak of skill I have ever demonstrated. Yes, with careful selection of teammates with meta builds and my own meta build play, a single victory was achieved in a long chain of defeats.

I am pleased with myself, I go to the forum and read: that I am apparently a shameful sucker, cowardly not wanting to lose thirty matches in a row by letting cool meta players into my lobbies.

In their fairy-tale world, a team filled with just anyone (in terms of builds) somehow appears to be able to provide participants with a win rate that allows them to climb ranks. But this is as far from the truth as it is possible in principle. Successful non-meta play is only possible with very strong teammates, or if the player is simply a god of clutches.

It turns out, according to their logic, either you are a clutch god - who broke through the very hell and now chill out relaxed at rank 40 with strong teammates, or: “sucker, loser, shut up, haha, skill issue”.

Yes, I am sure, and that single game of mine at rank 40, which happened thanks to Rvl’s help, only strengthened me in this: breaking through the thirties to rank forty is a more difficult task than playing at forty.

At the same time, they themselves are far from necessarily being clutch gods. Either they brazenly climb into rank 40 lobbies right away, flaunting their huge True Level, or they climb to rank 40 at the beginning of the season, on a powerful upward stream of skilled players regaining their rank 40 Assignment Rank, or they play not with randoms.

So it turns out that some elitists who have climbed to rank forty mock those who failed to do so, for trying to do so by playing meta and selecting meta teammates.

That is, they mock not even the current achievements of such players, but already in advance their future achievements.

If specifically about me, I have come to the conclusion that I am capable of playing at rank 40 by playing meta (even if on a role requiring minimal skill), but if I play non-meta and don’t select meta teammates, I will inevitably end up at rank 21, with frequent defeats even at rank 20. There it is, the real difference between meta and non-meta. And to mock non-meta play of those who, even with meta, did not reach rank 40, is absolutely unethical. It is roughly like an athlete from a strong team mocking an athlete from a weak team, not only for his current achievements with that team, but also for possible future ones
