The Omnissiah’s Forge: A 4‑Step Endgame Quest

To remind the good folks at FatShark for the hundredth time about their promise to rework the crafting system for curios, well, what’s the point?

But right now, I’m not talking about that though it’s close. Spoiler alert: before you say “this is too OP, can’t do that” and so on this is just a concept and a suggestion (which you can tweak/rewrite however you like). One that FatShark won’t even read, let alone send for review. So it’s only for idiots like me and you, who for some reason keep suggesting ways to improve/change the game, knowing full well it’s a pointless endeavor.

So, as you probably know, we have the following elements in the game that I’ll be talking about:

  1. Melk

  2. The godforsaken and ABSOLUTELY useless, failed Expeditions mode

  3. Mortis Trials

  4. Endgame in the form of Havoc (talking 35+ here)

Let’s start with point 1. Half of Melk’s contracts are physically impossible to complete on Havoc, due to the absence of items needed like scriptures and grimoires. Also, the shop itself lost its relevance when the updated crafting system came out. You might say it’s a way for new players to get a “good” item without the long weapon mastery grind. No, I completely disagree. Not only can you buy at most one item for a fully completed set of contracts, but it also won’t be perfect in stats and perks (I’m talking 80/80 and 4/4). Essentially, the shop is just a quick way to get curios, and after that, you forget about it. Sure, I get that contracts like “kill X enemies” are decent, but I won’t nitpick. Their lineup has needed an update for a long time.

What if, alongside updating the contract lineup, Melk gave out a quest that starts a chain of quests, and upon completion, we’d get the ability to craft a unique weapon (or curio, but let’s talk weapon for now)? Let’s talk about that. Roughly speaking, with 4 characters per week, you’d get 10k of his currency (VERY ROUGHLY). At Melk’s shop, you could buy a permit to upgrade a weapon for, say, 30k (like all subsequent prices, this is approximate you can adjust it yourself). Not too much, not too little. Once you get this item, you head to the Shrine of the Omnissiah, where you need to buy another permit this time for artifact hunting. Let’s make the cost 200k Plasteel and 50k Diamantine.

Now point 2. A great mode that could have been a reason to farm something for weapon/curio upgrades, or a new currency but in the end, I don’t have enough words to describe how poorly it’s been handled, especially for those who know about the Chaos Wastes, and even if not, it’s still terrible. So here, we’d farm those Tech Remnants. But this is mandatory only on difficulty 5 not 2, not 3, but 5. Sorry, I don’t know how many you get per mission since I don’t play this mode, let’s say 5k (correct me if I’m wrong). Not only do we need to enter a map with X amount of Tech Remnants, but we also need to complete all 3 stages of each map (or 5 if allowed). In the bunker, you buy an artifact piece with the remnants. You need 3 pieces for a full one, and you need 5 artifacts total so you’d need at least several games to collect all 5. Total cost could be around 30-50k for all.

Then point 3.
Suddenly Mortis. Since we want to craft a unique item of great power, what better way to test that power than in the Psykhanium? Two options here: either by some miracle FatShark hears that we need an endless waves mode, or not. Either way, you need to play at least 100 waves in this mode. And like with the Wastelands exclusively difficulty 5.

Almost done point 4.
Havoc. Since we’re making an extremely powerful weapon (move over, Inferno Staff), we’ve proven our theoretical strength in the Psykhanium now it’s time to prove it in practice. Off to endgame. Two requirements. First and main: you must have a TRUE host rank of at least 35. Second: win at least 10 games on Havoc 35+. You could set it to 40, but to be fair, there’s not that big a difference, and the game can already surprise you. Plus, this is more than enough of a filter for players.

After completing all 4 points, you turn in the quest to Melk, and he gives you some “pretty MEGA-SUPER INQUISITION PERMIT” to craft a weapon. Then you go craft it and you’ll say, “That’s it? So easy?”
No. This permit is for crafting ONE item. To keep it sane, you could tie it to a weapon family — say, you want knives then apply this item in the mastery section.

So, what do we get after all this? Lots of options. Since I assume you’re not dumb, you can refine/adjust/redo this concept based on what you’ve read and your own ideas. My version for now:

First the ability to push one stat to 100%. Wait, don’t shout. You do this by taking from another stat. You have DAMAGE and MOBILITY; you want 100% damage you lower mobility. Which stat you boost and which one you drain you choose. You could even drop one stat to 0, but that’s your call.

Regarding perks there are two options, maybe even three or more (need an Ogryn to count). First: combined perks that give 50% of their original value: +2.5% crit chance and +5% crit damage, taking up one perk slot.
Or you can double a perk not 5% crit chance, but 10%. Or duplicate perks: +2 stamina and +2 stamina. But if you pick doubled or duplicate perks, they can’t be used again in another slot though you could use a combined perk.
Or you could add a third perk slot, but it’d only give 50% value, and you can’t put doubled/combined perks in it.

Blessings could work the same way.

And now you’re saying, “This is way too strong - we already have balance issues, and that’s not even mentioning the Inferno Staff” (with all the crying and tears from the offended). But here’s the question: for what difficulty is this too strong? Auric where you just need to find W and Shift+LMB? Havoc which they keep making easier? What difficulty are you balancing for? This isn’t about balance it’s about adding fun and build variety. Objectively, all recent balance patches have been driven by Havoc, and maybe before asking to nerf X because it’s too strong, we should ask to bring back the old Crusher spawns (which are now an endangered species). Increase the Havoc horror points so there are more enemies. Fix boss spawns so there aren’t 5+ at once (though with this weapon, maybe it’s fine). Alright, I’m getting carried away.

My suggestion is a weapon upgrade system overhaul one that would buff less popular weapons, increase player retention, force players to play more than just the one mode they like, push them out of their comfort zone, and let us finally get that “red” weapon from Vermintide. It’s a viable concept yes, it’s rough and messy but it can be refined and fleshed out. But why bother? We’d rather have our hundredth discussion about how some talent or item is too strong because everyone’s got an opinion on that. But something everyone can agree on? We won’t discuss that.
