QuantumRage • 2025-12-20 17:00:24

have you tried a single threaded benchmark to see if the same thermal spike occurs?
most modern cpu’s will have a boost/turbo mode for single threaded workloads. you have a temp sensor on each cpu core itself, but whatever you are using for monitoring temps may simply reporting the highest temp any core rather then averaging temps across all cores, pushing a single core to 5 ghz, may spike the perceived temperature more then pushing 8 cores to 4.2 ghz for example.
this is quite normal behavour for a cpu however and your temps are WELL within specifications,

turbo mode allows a single thread of your cpu to spike briefly which helps when you need a short burst of speed to do a task, turbo mode will only activate if your cpu temps and power draw limits are in check.

i have personally never heard of a cooler “lagging”, i could see that as a reasonable assumption however if you are running your pump at a very low speed or even off. then sure, a burst work load like this “might” be able to heat the water so much that before the pump kicks on.

you may want to double check to make sure your pump is always running, if you built the computer yourself (or brought a pre built by who knows where) see is your pump is connected to fan header, your motherboard may be treating it as a fan and turning it off entirely. based on your fan curve settings etc. that wouldn’t be ideal your pump should always be running 24/7

fan speed on this should not matter for this particular scenario as it is not a long workload where your entire loop is reaching equilibrium. that burst workload will only be heating water, and then dissipated rad/ fans later on.

I did a little testing with my 360 Arctic AIO and 9800X3D. As I lowered the pump speed I did see an increase in temps when quitting the game. Not nearly as big as OP though. At 10%, which is a bad idea, I was only seeing a bump from 50C to 60C when quitting the game.

Are you using the single 4-Pin fan plug, or the one that splits it into 3 separate plugs. Also what MB do you have? Some motherboards have a dedicated pump ‘fan’ header that should be used if available. Not all fan headers are the same either. Most non-pump headers are 1 amp at 12 volt. the 420 Arctic can pull 1.45 amps. 0.35 for the pump, 0.35 for each of the fans, and 0.05 for the VRM fan. Pump headers aren’t really standardized but they typically rated at or above 2 amps.

Personally I run the VRM fan at 100%, the pump at 80% and the fans vary by temp between 20% and 100%. My system is on the other side of a wall so noise isn’t a concern for me though…
