BizarreSalp • 2026-08-12 17:42:26

You’re still wrong on that in this thread, though. They were well-designed, the best shielded enemies in a 'tide game, and I’ll re-iterate that from scratch here.

Other 'tide-game shield enemies have had major problems. Shield Skaven and Shield Marauders were not a serious issue; their shields broke easily to most weapons, and there’s the circumventing-the-shield mechanic of the double push.

Shieldvermin were easily countered with the “open up” pushes when alone, but groups of them could easily let the still-guarding ones eat swings aimed at one you had opened up. This is why they had the rather crude counter of “some weapons just invalidate them” (musket and flail, come to mind) added in.

Bulwarks went too far in the tanky side; you cannot force them to open up. The imbalance of some things in DT mean that some things invalidate them, but groups of Bulwarks can create situations that are annoyingly hard and genuinely take a very long time to break open, like when multiple Bulwarks are covering each other.

I stopped playing VT2 not long after Shielded Chaos Warriors were added, but I found they just got beat down pretty quickly.

Vanguards solved most of the problems of prior iterations. There is the same issue of some skills/weapons just invalidating them, but that is a general issue with those weapons. For every other weapon, they were actually extremely fair, the best iteration so far. You can break their shields, but it’s not easy, so it’s a trade-off to not take an anti-shield weapon. You can extremely easily just dodge behind them after they go into a block stance, meaning you can remove them, but you have to do something other than just “m1 spam”.

But, as a last ditch “out” for players to prevent the issue of groups covering each other too well, you CAN destroy the shields, meaning that you are not hard trapped by them and have a chance to fight your way out.

If people are having trouble with them, if they find themselves unable to deal with them efficiently, that is on them. I’m sorry, but like . . . this isn’t a huge skill. The challenge is commiting it to muscle memory, and that’s it. Yes, groups can kinda cover each other more, which is why reducing spawn numbers might have been reasonable, but even with a horde - it’s a simple gaming sequence of figuring out which is positioned in a way you can take advantage of. That’s all.

If you’re getting boxed in by them, that means you made a mistake in positioning. Sometimes this can be forced onto you, but these enemies are, like all other enemies, easy to kite.

So this is why complaints of not liking them swivel between “they annoy me” and “they’re too tanky”. If they’re annoying you, I think it’s likely that you’re just not used to them yet and you were used to everything else, so asking a new skill of you feels like a lot in your game. That is normal, but it leads the game to stagnation, because it means you oppose new things.

If you think they’re too tanky, then you’re just not approaching them right. Because I was having a fine time, on my support Zealot, killing them with two body shots from Tac Axe, Combat Axe, and on both the saber Dueling Sword and the Heavy Sword. Oh, and with Combat Knife and Shock Maul (the generic one) on Skitty.
