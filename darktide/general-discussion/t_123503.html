played skitarii release with new player friends and here’s some of the issues i noted

  1. nothing in the game seems to actually inform you that you can swap marks on weapons on the fly, the button is there but “marks” isnt a mechanics new players are actually ever told about
  2. the purpose of melk is nebulous now that you don’t have to unlock blessings anymore
  3. the event tab on melk showing past events is confusing and makes people think this is content thats available to players right now because it’s not clear enough these are past events
  4. a lot of penances in the game still refer to auric heresy and auric damnation, which is ultra confusing for new players who don’t know about the past mission board design
  5. many weapons get exponentially cucked by having low level stat bars in relation to other weapons, which makes them seem way worse than they actually are in comparison
  6. the mastery system is mostly unexplained menus that seem obvious if you followed the games development, but is just utterly confusing and doesn’t seem worth engaging with for new players beyond basic upgrading of weapons
  7. when starting out the gameplay experience is sort of ruined by experienced players speedrunning missions because of the dlc release, and that’s 90% of the playerbase
  8. no randoms are ever willing to do or even wait for the skull puzzles due to aforementioned dynamic
  9. curio crafting is very weirdly uninvolved with all the other systems in the game, and getting them is an issue without having 20k+ melk coins saved up

that’s all i can think of right now, might add more when i remember what else i/they/we experienced. many of these things seem obvious if you know, but a new player misses them because the game has many menus with a lot of counterintuitive design at this point.

Frish • 2026-07-03 02:49:12

The mastery system is actively harmful to new players, be they new to the game or to the class.

At a point where a player should be encouraged to experiment, they are incentivised to specialise. Weapons without mastery are inferior to those with, and the player becomes dependent on RNG to compensate (Brunt’s, Emperor’s Gift).

Oh, you just unlocked level 300 weapons? Too bad the shiny new weapon you unlocked is capped at 100 power and feels like a herring in the difficulty you’ve just progressed to.

Oh, you want to boost it up just so you can play around with it? Let’s sap your resources (not everyone has unlimited) and valuable time by making you craft, upgrade, and discard weapons one-by-one (OK you can discard in bulk).

I get that it introduces some progression, and was tacked on at a later date, but imo it fails several design principles that lead it to being an active deterrent for players. Also allowing high-level gear into low difficulties contributes to a watered-down quickplay experience…
