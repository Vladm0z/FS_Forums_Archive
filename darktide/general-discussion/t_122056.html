BUT if we are talking about realism daggers were very useful. If you’re not using a bludgeoning weapon daggers were better against armored targets. If you’re using a 4 foot sword the kinetic force from your arms and wrist is longer and the blade is much more likely to get deflected in stabs (slashes don’t work against armor) and the sword is more likely to even bend.

A dagger is short, very rigid and doesn’t lose so much force from your arms (the source of all the kinetic power behind all melee weapons) and can break armor. Hence, the rondel in medieval warfare (not that I am expert).

Funnily enough I would say if you wanted realism, dagger should be a great anti-armor awful defensive/horde clear weapon that lets you be mobile. It wouldn’t be as anti-armor as hammers and clubs and such, but it would be better than swords for sure.
