You might argue that in most cases it’s fine, but it depends on the viewing angle. From other perspectives, it might appear brighter, but in this spot, from all angles, it’s this bad.

This is caused by a combination of eye adaptation/auto-exposure and post-process volume manipulations.

For example:

  • In Room #1, if lights are on, looking into Room #2 (lights off) shows nothing.

  • Entering Room #2 triggers exposure changes, revealing the interior without blinding you with Room #1’s brightness.

Is here any better solution? Yeah, it calls path tracing, which mirrors light realistically. For example:

A box near a door gets illuminated when you open the door from the outside.

You close the door - box loses light.

You then open from the inside → box gets much less light than in the step #1.
