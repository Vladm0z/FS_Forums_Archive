Hello,

I would like to ask whether Fatshark’s position on an end-of-mission scoreboard has changed since the last statements on the subject.

I am asking this because the arguments commonly used against scoreboards are often presented as established facts, although I am not aware of evidence demonstrating the claimed causal relationship between scoreboards and toxicity or poor teamwork.

There are, in particular, three claims that come up repeatedly:

1. “Scoreboards make the community toxic.”

There is an important distinction between a scoreboard being used by toxic players and a scoreboard causing toxic behaviour.

A player insulting another player after a failed mission because that player’s damage was lower does not demonstrate that the scoreboard caused the hostility. The mission failure itself provides an obvious source of frustration, and the scoreboard may simply provide the player with a convenient metric with which to justify an already existing blame attribution.

Research on toxic behaviour in multiplayer games tends to identify a much broader range of factors, including individual characteristics, motivations, competitive context, social norms, and the ways in which players justify aggressive behaviour.

For example:

Lemercier-Dugarin et al. (2021) examined empathy, impulsivity and gaming motivations as predictors of aggressive behaviour.

Zsila et al. (2022) examined prevalence, risk factors and consequences of toxic behaviour and identified factors such as age and competitive gaming context.

Tan & Chen (2022) specifically investigated intra-team aggressive behaviour and the excuses/neutralisation strategies used by players engaging in such behaviour.

None of these studies establish a causal relationship of the form “scoreboard → toxic behaviour.”

This does not prove that scoreboards can never contribute to toxicity. It does, however, mean that “scoreboards cause toxicity” should not be treated as an established empirical fact without evidence demonstrating that causal relationship.

2. “Scoreboards lead to worse teamwork.”

This claim also seems more complicated than it is sometimes presented.

A scoreboard can certainly encourage players to compare their individual performance. But individual performance feedback and poor teamwork are not synonymous.

In fact, an end-of-mission scoreboard can provide players with useful information about their individual contribution to a successful team effort, but also simply a fun way of reminiscing about the mission:*
*
“We completed the mission, and this is what I contributed.”

This is particularly relevant in Darktide, where different classes and builds contribute in very different ways. Damage alone obviously does not capture the entirety of a player’s contribution. However, that is an argument for designing a scoreboard carefully, not necessarily for removing performance feedback altogether.

A scoreboard could potentially display a broader range of statistics: specials killed, elites killed, damage dealt, revives, rescues, coherency-related information, etc. It could also be presented only after the mission, rather than turning the mission itself into a competition.

There is therefore a substantial difference between:

“A badly designed scoreboard can incentivise undesirable behaviour”

and

“Scoreboards inherently produce worse teamwork.”

The former is a reasonable design concern. The latter requires evidence.

3. “Green circle chasing” as an established player behaviour

This is perhaps the claim I find most problematic when it is used as an argument against scoreboards.

Players frequently refer to “green circle chasing” as though it were an established and measurable phenomenon in which players deliberately sacrifice team objectives in order to maximise their scoreboard statistics.

But how can we actually establish another player’s motivation?

If a player has exceptionally high damage or kill numbers, we cannot infer from those numbers alone that they were “chasing the green circle”. They might simply enjoy playing aggressively, be testing a build, have a particularly strong build for that situation, or simply be playing well.

Unless the player explicitly states that maximising the scoreboard was their motivation, attributing that motivation to them is speculation.

I am therefore not convinced that anecdotal reports of “green circle chasing” constitute evidence that scoreboards cause players to neglect teamwork.

4. A scoreboard can simply be fun

There is another reason to have a scoreboard that has little to do with competition or optimisation: looking at the numbers can simply be enjoyable.

After an intense Darktide mission, I might want to look at the statistics simply because I enjoyed the experience and want to see what happened.

It can be a way of reminiscing about the mission:

  • “We killed how many specialists?”
  • “No wonder that last section felt so chaotic.”
  • “I didn’t realise this build was doing that much damage.”

In that context, the scoreboard is not necessarily a competitive ranking at all. It is part of the post-game experience and can provide a shared point of reference for the players who just completed the mission together.

This is particularly relevant to a cooperative game. A scoreboard does not necessarily have to communicate “who was the best player?” It can simply communicate “what happened during that mission, and what did each of us contribute?”

That distinction seems important when evaluating the potential benefits and drawbacks of such a system.

What I would therefore like to know

Has Fatshark conducted, commissioned, or reviewed any empirical research specifically examining whether the presence of a scoreboard in Vermintide/Darktide actually increases:

  • toxic behaviour,
  • intra-team aggression,
  • blame attribution,
  • teamplay failures,
  • or mission failures?

If such research exists, I would genuinely be interested in seeing it.

If it does not, has Fatshark’s position changed since the last public statement made by Hedge on the subject?

I fully understand that Fatshark may have good design reasons for not implementing a scoreboard. My question is specifically whether the often-repeated claims that scoreboards cause toxicity, damage teamwork, or create widespread “green circle chasing” are still part of Fatshark’s current reasoning, and if so, what empirical evidence supports those claims.

Thank you for taking the time to clarify this.

And people, please keep the conversation civil. There is nothing to be gained to imply ulterior motives in those asking for a scoreboard and insinuating whatever bad characteristics, nor does it help to do the same to people who oppose the scoreboard.
