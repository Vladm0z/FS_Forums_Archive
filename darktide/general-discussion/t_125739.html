f8fqgfm • 2026-09-21 02:59:10

I think a clear, attainable difficulty ceiling really does create a certain kind of psychological suggestion. Let me use World of Warcraft’s Mythic+ mode as an example again: because Mythic+ has no difficulty cap, many WoW players who aren’t that interested in pushing the limits will say, “Oh, once I’ve completed the achievements and reached a certain key level, that’s enough. Going higher gives me almost no additional psychological feedback or gear rewards, so there’s no need to take part in an endless arms race of skill and loadouts.”

In Darktide’s current situation, you have to clear Havoc level 40 to get all the rewards, and top players are also running level 40. Yet level 40 isn’t actually so difficult that most skilled players simply cannot clear it, so many players who are neither very strong nor very weak will want to make a push and get through. If clearing Havoc level 30 were enough to get all the rewards, while the very top players cleared, say, Havoc level 50, then for ordinary skilled players, the in-game rewards and competitive payoff from challenging Havoc above level 30 would drop sharply, and they would therefore be less keen to choose a small number of meta loadouts.

Incidentally, the new version of World of Warcraft has an obvious trend of lowering the barrier; it’s said that doing Mythic+ achievements in the current version is much less difficult than before. So this level 30 could be adjusted to level 25, or whatever—it doesn’t need to be high.

For those players who are absolutely unwilling to lower the achievement threshold, I want to say: if you’re that competitive, you’ll get even more satisfaction in the revised environment where most players stop at level 30 while you push all the way to level 50. Being exclusionary is not a good thing.
