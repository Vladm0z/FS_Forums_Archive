Actually, I think this is a better designed penance than you are giving it credit for.

I don’t think most players are likely to deliberately leave a teammate downed for the sake of the penance. (I believe that griefing can be discouraged by disconnecting if someone seems to be doing it, in which case you just leave them with a bot who can’t be looted). If I’m not getting someone up in Expeditions mode, it’s because they’ve got themselves isolated and going for them is dangerous and a huge time sink.

Remember, players can still die without someone toxically refusing to get them up; it’s not that hard to run out of wounds. It’s a penance I expect people to complete passively with time.

Heck, if anything, this penance encourages your team to stay close rather than leave you on your own so your loot is lost if you die.

Also, the risk required also provides a bit more incentive to push onto the tougher later stages, which I’ve already found are often being overlooked - a lot of people people prefer to go in, hit the first zone, then extract. The game mode itself doesn’t do a good job of giving you a reason to risk your entire progress by going on rather than just getting out early and then queueing again.

Only the penances and completionism provide much pressure to go through into later stages.

DrHammer • 2026-03-18 17:10:40

There are two wolves living inside the mind of fatshark when it comes to penances.

One wants penances to be tests of skill, one-time feats of effort that players have to actively work towards, or else be very lucky.

The other wants penances that you slowly unlock overtime, and that you don’t actively pursue. Penances are a mark of commitment, not skill.

But the two wolves don’t talk to each other, which occasionally results in penances that work when unlocked passively without ever thinking about what happens when they’re pursued actively. (For example, encouraging players to run a single map over-and-over)
