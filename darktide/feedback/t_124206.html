NeilDaRat • 2026-07-19 00:58:18

Havoc mode is in dire need of a rework.

I have 9.2k hours in Verm, and 1.7k in darktide, but at this point I can no longer continue to play the game with the current state of havoc mode.

The previous rotations completely killed my enjoyment for this game by forcing blight spreads for months on end, forcing Chasm Station and Chasm Logistratum into every map playlist which are all extremely limited in the first place, and balance changes displaying a complete lack of understanding of game balance by the developers.

The game has been out for full release for years now, I think it’s finally time to revisit havoc mode and consider providing loyal and endgame players an enjoyable way to experience your game.

I have a few suggestions for havoc mode that are not perfect, but would be VERY EASY to implement for Fatshark, and would be an objective upgrade to the mode that I think any endgame player would agree feels quite undercooked:

  1. Open all maps in the playlist. This should have been done from the start. By providing a very small limited pool of maps for havoc rotations, you effectively force the people who spend the most time on the game to have the least varied experience while playing the game. The design is baffling and unless there are severe technological issues preventing this from happening, this should be implemented ASAP.

  2. REVISIT HAVOC MODIFIERS. - The modifiers for havoc are a failure in design. No havoc players search for specific modifiers or log on to have fun with specific modifiers, modifiers serve as an annoyance and players often interact with modifiers by avoiding the bad modifiers or prioritizing the least annoying or least intrusive to their gameplay experience. While I know revisiting modifiers is likely too much to ask (I assume doing ANY amount of work with this game is too much work at this point with my current faith in fatshark), an alternative would be to OPEN ALL MODIFIERS during each rotation. This would allow for many more combinations of modifiers and prevent the current situation where 4 players will often have 2 or 3 people with the exact same map and modifiers. This would also allow for interesting modifier combinations and make the endgame experience feel much less limiting.

There are many glaring issues with the game and with balance, but I think tackling the issues above would actually help give some goodwill back to the endgame players who feel completely neglected and disrespected by the current handling of havoc mode. I think these would also be extremely easy to implement from a technical level, and I’m curious to hear from the community how they would like to improve havoc mode or the overall endgame experience.

Modifiers for maps are such a fantastic idea, one that I suggested should be added since VT2, but the ones they’ve done are just not fun.

Hunting Grounds - Everyone is tired of this. They’ve gone on about it at length. The bugginess of dogs doesn’t help. I do feel it could get an update where there’s increased chances of Pack Masters spawning, aside from normal Monster spawns. Sort of make it a mini “Monster Specials”, which would be fun.

Power Supply Interruption - Anti-fun and literally gives me, personally, a headache from eye strain. I would be happy if I NEVER had to play this one again, and it’s the only modifier that will make me leave games sometimes. I get that it wants to be moodier, and I think it succeeds in that! But it’s just so dark, and so you literally just see darkness in front of you the entire match until enemies appear there. Especially when someone is abusing the Flame Staff, that’s when the contrast of light and dark really starts to hurt my eyes.

Pox Gas - It’s a good idea, but the implementation is awful. It drags matches out because you inevitably get stuck waiting for cycles of gas (that last far too long) and then when they’re gone you get hordes that prevent pushing. It also severely punishes some classes more than others.
The fields of pox gas are also sometimes insanely large, covering huge areas.
These need to be sporadic and have pre-set layouts that differ. They should force players to use areas that are typically bypassed.

Ventilation Purge - This is not a very fun one, but it’s not that bad. Hordes of snipers can create situations that feel unwinnable (which is, admittedly, a team comp and skill issue), but I have less annoyance with it than others.

The last three have another major problem; if you spawn into a match in darkness, tox gas, or sometimes the fog, you can be completely disoriented on where you are in the map or even which direction you’re supposed to be going. And since you’re often filling in a slot, that means that the situation is bad. You always inherit the god-awful bot’s conditions, so you’re likely to be at a quarter health on your last wound, sometimes without grenades or even a loaded ranged weapon.
And then you don’t know where you are! Just a sea of green, since besides the Tox Gas anuses that fart everywhere there are probably Tox Bombers that no one is killing covering the entire map in toxins.

The game would be a hell of a lot better if these issues could be connected not to the whole run but to AREAS of the map. “Oh no, this area has some toxic-gas spewing anuses! We’ll have to be careful!” Or the power is out in a specific area.

There are some good ones. Monstrous Specials is something I love to play, along with the “no ammo pickups, only Scab” mutator. It can be fun to take your chunkiest build and grind your way through them.

Events have produced some interesting modifiers, too. I would love to see those return and appear randomly. Random side objective of destroying a cultist pillar that summons a boss or Captain. A box of extra stimms for everyone!
Free the modifiers!

Being a VT player for years (I was in the first closed beta for 2, had a lot of hours in 1 as well), I think the thing that has hurt this series the most is adding in stuff like talent trees and power scaling on players.

It’s just too much to balance even for game companies with far higher resources. We can see this in any MP game, where they will struggle for the entire lifetime of the game to make balance (and, well, we can’t overlook the trend of intentional imbalance as a cheap way to create more engagement over time).

It also widens the gulf between optimized builds and inefficient builds.

Which, in some games, optimizing your build almost is the gameplay. World of Warcraft is the pre-eminent example across gaming, in my mind.

The 'tide games, since VT1, are about teamwork and individual skill expression in the gameplay loop.

In a future 'tide-style game, I would hope to see no talent trees and no power scaling with leveling/gear. Customization being relegated solely to loadout (with minimal modifiers on them, simply well-developed movesets) and cosmetics.

Then, focus on allowing players to tailor difficulty, scaling health, spawn numbers, harshness of the director . . .

But some IGN reviewer would probably go “uh there’s no sense of ~progression~” because they’re talentless hacks. Just like after the first VT.
