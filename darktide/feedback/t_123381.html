OK, let’s broaden the narrative picture a little bit - I know precious little about the technical aspects of WH40K, but if Lexicanum is anything to go by, it’s still not feasible choice, since (article on Radium weapons):

Their purpose is to not only slay the enemy with a blast of radioactive force, but to also render the battlefield a deadly rad-waste.

Now do you want to make Tertium hive a ‘rad waste’? Because as the story goes, the warband’s purpose is to liberate Atoma of M6 and Dregs, whereas scorch-earthing it would go against the narrative. Radium weapons I imagine are iconic and FS not implementing them off the get go seems to be a rational choice that’s supposed to tie in with mainly the story that they want to tell. This is the type of weapon you employ in Skitarii-only deployments, against foe you don’t reason with either for resources, or territory.

Roibr:

Your Argument falls apart with ive Scum if you played him or seen him anyway.

No it doesn’t - friendly fire isn’t out of the question, and damage to infrastructure can be justified, but if you are rendering a forge unsuable due to displaced measures or people of your jolly bunch of soldiers sick or dead (radium weapons), then you’ve got a problem. How do you justify that, as a storyteller?

Toxin-related poisonings could be explicably justified - the Mourningstar has a dedicated Magos Biologis on-board, so individual cases of people getting sick can be treated; and on missions themselves the acolytes are finding either deployments of cure-all stimms or functional medical stations, so healing individuals from “accidents” is guaranteed. You can’t justify healing an irradiated person with these measures, especially if the count can go up very easily - it just won’t work. Rejects may be cannon fodder, but losing your best operatives to that? Tough - no - incredibly tough sell.

The same applies to your suggestion about reworking Ablative wards - it’s incredulous that you’d imagine irradiated mecha-soldiers fighting alongside non-shielded human-adjacent individuals is a rational choice for story-driving purposes. For the faction’s fan-service maybe it would be, but that’s not the story the writer wants to tell, and thank goodness they don’t.

That’s not to say story shouldn’t sometimes give way to gameplay, but FS generally try to tie the two as close together as possible. Friendly fire is an example of that - you probably could make the gameplay more hardcore and have friendly fire deal damage, but it wouldn’t be a fun experience in the end. (Which could be why FF has become only an auditory disturbance following ~Alpha/Beta period of development or so, but voice lines for it remain so, since they enable some additional dialog triggers for the devs to use.)
