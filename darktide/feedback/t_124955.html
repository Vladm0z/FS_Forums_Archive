I think we all know by now that the DLC classes don’t seem to be getting additional items in the Commodore’s Vestures premium store.
The most popular running theory is that Games Workshop is incredibly strict with their IP, and have not approved any additonal cosmetics for Arbites, Hive Scum and Skitarii. There could be some truth to that, I think.
However, I have a different theory. My own personal theory is that Tencent/Fatshark do not believe it worthwhile/do not have the dev resources to allocate towards making cosmetics for these classes. As someone who has worked briefly on a game’s development team, I suspect it could be this angle, to some degree.
After all, we’re talking spending the dev time/resources to make premium optional cosmetics for a premium optional DLC class in an already premium game. That’s three layers deep of expected purchase to sell cosmetics for the DLC classes, and it could very well be that they consider the number of players who’d buy in to cosmetics for a buy-in class wouldn’t justify spending the time to make them. It could be a combination of reasons as well, but that’s what I think is a likely reason.

If that is true, then I think a potential solution is relatively simple: Make several of the cosmetics that already exist for Veteran/Zealot/Psyker also available to be equipped by Arbitrator/Hive Scum. (I am leaving Ogryn and Skitarii out of this equation because their models are structured differently and can’t share cosmetics, whereas Veteran/Zealot/Psyker/Arbites/Hive Scum all use the same ‘standard’ body type and can share items)
This has actually already been done, in fact, to a small degree. The most prominent example of this is Accessories that go on the back, such as this Vox Caster item:

example11920×1247 353 KB

As you can see, it says it can be equipped by the five ‘standard skeleton’ classes, and this is true of basically all Accessory items that can be worn by any of them.
Another example would be the basic face-augmetic items, like this one:

example21920×1141 299 KB

Notably, there was a minor controversy on release of Arbites, in which this item was equippable right away, then shortly thereafter removed from being able to be used by Arbites. After outcry, the ability to equip it was restored, and it then went on to be used by Hive Scum too. This proves that having items be multi-class is not out of the question, so that’s why I think it’s a potential solution to players’ desires to be able to customize Arbites and Hive Scum with more options.

Before I launch into a big long list of exactly what I think should be multi-class (and I must apologize up front, but it will be QUITE a big long list, I want to be exhaustive), I first want to provide what I think are some of the most egregious and hilarious examples of items that exist in game that, in my opinion, should be able to be equipped by Hive Scum.

Now, to begin with, I want to talk about the Hive Scum first. I think it’s the class that thematically could have the most shared with it from the available selection in-game. Many of the premium cosmetics in-game, especially from the Veteran, are ganger-themed already.
Although, I also tried to consider the class’s ‘identity’, so to speak, so I avoided Veteran items/sets that seem too clean-cut ‘militaristic’, Zealot items that are overly ‘religious’, and almost all Psyker items are unfitting (which I will explain why later).
What I was aiming for, are items/sets that as well as being ganger-themed, seem ‘scruffier’, looted, salvaged, stolen, street-level, or would otherwise contribute towards a more ‘mercenary’ look for the Hive Scum, as in the Necromunda setting upon which they are based, the term ‘Hive Scum’ more accurately applies to hired mercenaries moreso than gangers, though from the perspective of the Inquisition making one an acolyte, there isn’t much difference.

For Psyker, there is a major issue, which I mentioned previously.

That being, that by the very cosmetic design and nature of Psyker, they always have a ‘psychic hood’ around their neck, and almost all of their Head items are designed to sit atop it. Without the collar, their Head items would float above the shoulders, which obviously would be inappropriate.

Additionally, most Psyker body items are themed specifically for Psyker, containing a lot of tubing and plating meant to help Quell the Warp energies they channel.

As such, there is only one single set I consider suitable for Psyker, and a very small handful of Upper and Lower Body items from sets. Once I have shown them all, I will explain exactly why.

Arbitrator. There is only really one cosmetic set that I think would be appropriate to be multi-class for the Arbites, and that is the Enforcer set for Veteran, Officer Breen Millendon (seen in default and red):

EnforcerSet11050×1305 266 KB
EnforcerSet21065×1302 266 KB

I figure that if an Imperial Guard Veteran, an Ogryn etc can be able to be dressed as an Enforcer, an Arbites should be too, as they’re arguably more adjacent to what an ‘Enforcer’ is than any of those. One of the Psyker’s personality types references being an ex-Enforcer, but that’s about it.

Beyond that, though, no real other complete cosmetic sets are, in my opinion, suitable for Arbites. Arbites has a pretty noteable visual ‘theme’, and many in-game dialogue lines reference this, especially Ogryns calling them ‘Plate-Face’, so I think that visual identity shouldn’t be detracted from as much.

However, I think given that the Arbites in-game are being requisitioned by the Inquisition, and possibly some outstanding members being formally adopted as Acolytes, it should be within the bounds of imagination/lore to suggest they might customize their Upper Body somewhat to reflect their new Inquisitorial Acolyte status, while still keeping their Head and Leg Armour to show their previous skillset and identity.
