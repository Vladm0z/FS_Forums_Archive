i’ve seen an analogy on this recently that was very good.

let’s say you rent a house. you rent it from a big building company that builds houses to rent them out.
when you rent this house, you notice the roof isn’t waterproof. water drips into your living room.

obviously, this is a problem. you will obviously voice this problem to the company. they will obviously need your feedback, or they’ll never know the roof isn’t waterproof. they need to listen to you, because this is a real problem.

now, what do they do next? do they ask YOU what to do? no, they’re experienced in this. they know how to fix a leaky roof. they have their own tradesmen in their company who know how to fix it. they don’t ask YOU to fix it, because you’re not an architect or professional. you’re the consumer. your feedback matters, coming up with the solution isn’t your role. maybe the issue is not even the roof, maybe the leak is not actually a leak but condensation in a lamp fitting.

the same is true for game development. they should listen to player feedback yes. but fixing these issues? do not listen to players. they’re not devs, their ideas on how to fix it will be bad and purely speculative. YOU are the professionals, not the players.

in the analogy i presented, letting players decide what should change is akin to letting the guy who rented the house decide how to fix it when he notices water on the ceiling. only a madman would let this happen, and it’s highly unprofessional.

and btw, this needs to shape how fatshark sees feedback. “power sword is trash, buff it” might not actually fix anyones issue with it, which could be the moveset. so when fatshark sees “power sword is underpowered” then the solution to this sentiment may not actually be buffing it, but maybe it’s adding more variants with different movesets. the complaint is the leak, the fix to the leak is what professional experience and many iterations of testing will lead fatshark to. so when people for example say

vanguards are WAY TOO TANKY NERF THEM PLEASE!!!

then maybe their perception that vanguards are too tanky isn’t wrong. it could be contextually caused by how the rest of the game is a room clearing warframe experience to these players, where killing elites will spawn aoe dots that will kill more elites that then spread the dots further. that’s a real issue, and it’s jarring, but the solution to it might not be making vanguards into that, hint hint.

The problem with Vanguards is not exactly that they are tanky - so are Bulwkars, or Crushers, and I never had issues with them and defended them from those who said “they are too hard to kill” (they are not). It’s just that Vanguards are too many and they are too often mixed with the regular chaff ruining the experience we love DT for - crunching waves of heretic cannon fodder in one uninterrupted treadmill of carnage.

Different types of foes serve different purposes. The purpose of chaff is to inconvenience you a bit by swamping and distracting you - and die quickly. They are not supposed to drag as much your attention as elites. And elites are here to provide a bit of hardcore challenges, amidst the sea of chaff. That’s what combined into a typical experience of a hoard shooter.

And now you have a lot of highly-durable turds among chaff which interrupt your fun and slow down your chaff massacre drastically, for no reason at all. They are not fun to fight at all (unlike Crushers who are thrilling as you can fold in a moment when fighting them), they don’t have clear opening and weakspots like Bulwakrs (which are relatively easy to exploit and it’s actually fun to fool them around); no, you just need to poke them for a few seconds until they die. It’s irritating and boring.

The only reasonable option I see is to make them really fragile when hit from the back, and when their defense stance is already broken. And ofc let their heads be clear weakspots too (right now I can’t kill them by shooting to the head with dbarrel at pointblack, what kind of nonsense is that)?
