​2. ‘The colors only exist in the cosmetics menu, so they’re useless’

​Tell me you’ve never played a looter, RPG, or live-service game without telling me.

​Visual feedback loops inside inventory systems exist for player agency and collection status. When a player opens their loadout, tier colors immediately signal rarity, effort, and milestones at a single glance. Stripping that away under the guise of ‘menu convenience’ replaces standard cognitive design with a gray, lifeless wall of slop where a cosmetic unlocked in 5 minutes looks identical on paper to one that took a 50-hour grind.

​3. ‘Removing them loses nothing and makes sorting sensible’

​It loses clarity, contrast, and prestige.

​A flat, un-tiered menu is an information hierarchy nightmare. Color coding is the fastest visual shorthand human brains use to organize data. Removing color coding doesn’t ‘improve sorting’—it destroys visual grouping and forces players to manually read text labels to figure out what tier an item belongs to. You aren’t advocating for cleaner UI; you’re advocating for feature regression.

​Immediate Cognitive Feedback: Your brain processes color instantly. Tiered borders give immediate satisfaction when hovering over hard-earned gear.

​Collection Value: Players like filling out high-tier slots. Removing tiering removes the target goal for completionists.
