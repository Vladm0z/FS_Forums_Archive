DeathPointforYA • 2026-03-13 09:16:48

It seems we have fundamentally different perceptions of what makes a combat situation interesting. Keeping it the way it is now is simply unfair to the player because it allows for no counterplay.

I am not saying fodder spam should block the net. Trappers are taller than trash enemies, so if they shoot me over them, that is fair game. The problem is when there is an equally tall enemy between my position and the trapper. Hit reg on my side just damages the first enemy, while the trapper gets to do its thing in the background, which feels like BS.

And when it comes to snipers, they absolutely should not be able to shoot through hordes the way they do now, because they are just as tall as them. Once again, that creates a situation with no counterplay, because you cannot shoot them back.

In any case, they could scale the overall game back a bit too, but that is a different issue for another post.

Edit: Also I feel like you are conflating a mistake with a no counterplay situation to begin with. A mistake is when the player had an actionable response and failed, so getting punished for it is a logical outcome. But I am talking about cases where the response itself is invalidated.

I do not have a problem with trappers or snipers being dangerous. I have a problem with the game creating situations where their threat remains fully active while my ability to answer it is blocked by collision rules that only apply to me. That is not interesting because it is hard. It is frustrating because it removes counterplay.

Challenge is when the game gives me a real threat and I still have a fair chance to respond correctly. Otherwise it amounts to the game applying different spatial logic to the player and the enemy in the same situation.

And again, I am not saying fodder should fully protect me from specials. If a trapper shoots over trash enemies because she is taller than them, fine. If a sniper has a clean angle, fine. The issue is when there is no clean angle on my end, but apparently there is one on theirs because the rules stop mattering for enemy fire. That is where it stops being challenge and starts being BS.

If they want these enemies to stay threatening, then make them threatening through readable pressure, better positioning, faster punishes, or tighter timing windows. But “they can do it through bodies and you cannot” is not some sacred co-op design principle. It is just asymmetry that feels bad to play against.
