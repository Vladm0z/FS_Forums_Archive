oomf:

as apparently 4 ddr5 RAM sticks have instability issues!

4 sticks will always be less stable than 2 unfortunately. you may be able to get all 4 working if you down clock them to 5600 or use looser timings. In the BIOS, there should be several profiles for the memory to use. However 32 GB should be plenty for most things, if you really need 64 for other usages look to either down clock them or just get 2 sticks of 32 GB.

oomf:

It was spiking to 85c as soon as I loaded into the Mourningstar with all my settings cranked to max/uncapped fps/dlss on.
My best guess is all of these memory errors are probably from my CPU being stress tested at 100c 100% of the time im using my computer as a result of having little to no thermal paste, and a cooler that isnt cooling anything :’)

The memory controller for 13th gen Intels is on the CPU itself so that makes a lot of sense to start getting memory errors after it heats up. The heat isn’t helping but you may also have just lost the silicon lottery. There’s always variance chip to chip. I had a 5600X that I could easily get running with DDR4 3600 CL16. My buddy has a 5600X that can’t run 3600 CL16 no matter how hard we try.

Side note, since you have a 13th gen intel. Please make sure you updated the BIOS so it won’t cook itself to death. It sounds like your friend can help you check and update.

MeatShield01Regular • 2025-07-22 15:10:17

I would not do the Beta, F12 looks like the most recent non-beta option.

However reading the notes on the Beta I see “Introduce microcode 0x12F as it further improves system conditions that can potentially contribute to Vmin Shift Instability on Intel Core 13th and 14th Gen desktop-powered systems”. I would check back every few weeks to see when F13 is out of Beta and fully released, then wait another week or 2 to let some other people ‘go first’, then install it.

Also you should make sure you are using the 2 ‘best’ slots for the memory. DDR5_A2, and DDR5_B2. Page 4, and 15 from the manual. Those 2 are the correct configuration for dual channel and are the ‘most stable’ configuration.

oomf:

Do you think that permanent damage might already have been done to the CPU?

Hopefully not. My understanding is that having ‘too good’ cooling actually made things worse for the issue. Under certain circumstances when the 13/14 gens were cool enough, and needed more processing power they would increase the clock speed and voltage. However the voltage was being increased to an unsafe level and causing damage over time. But if you weren’t cooling it effectively it should never be in the circumstances to increase the voltage too much.
